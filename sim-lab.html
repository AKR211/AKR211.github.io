Welcome to the

Sim Lab

"Complex behavior can emerge from simple rules."

Scroll to explore

A simulation is an imitation of reality, built to mirror the physical world. But as we distill nature into pure mathematics and code, we create simulacra: standalone realities governed entirely by their own internal logic. These digital environments allow us to strip away the disturbances and limitations of the universe and we construct perfect lattices, manipulate fundamental forces, and define the absolute laws of massive multi-agent systems. Yet, the true beauty of these in silico experiments lies not in the control we exert, but in what happens when we let time run forward. By furnishing a system with nothing more than simple and logical local rules, we step back to observe the profound nature of emergent phenomena where chaotic agents organize themselves, and staggering complexity breathes life into these virtual worlds.

Animations

Simple demonstrations of various phenomena through computer simulations, bridging the abstract theory and mathematical equations with observable everyday experiences. From the simple motion of a bouncing ball to the intricate, chaotic switching of a double-scroll attractor, these simulations offer a glimpse into how mathematical rules dictate the logical as well as bizarre behaviors we encounter.

Inelastic bouncing of a ball in a closed box under gravity. Eventually, the ball comes to rest at the bottom of the box.

Motion of two pendulums coupled by a spring. The spring facilitates the transfer of energy between the pendulums. When the amplitude of one pendulum increases, the spring compresses and transfers energy to the other pendulum.

Motion of two double pendulums. They start from nearly identical initial conditions but their trajectories diverge over time, demonstrating the sensitive dependence on initial conditions characteristic of chaotic systems.

Self-similar structure of a fractal pattern. The koch curve has a haussdorf dimension of approximately 1.2619. As you zoom in, the pattern repeats itself at smaller scales.

Evolution of the double-scroll attractor, a strange attractor observed from a physical electronic chaotic circuit (generally, Chua's circuit).

Hysteresis is the dependence of a system’s output on both its current input and its past history of inputs. The stable point for the particle evolves differently depending on whether the parameter is increasing or decreasing.

Simulations

"All models are wrong, but some are useful." - George E.P. Box

Here we have live interactive environments for exploring complex systems through agent-based models. Play around with the parameters to observe emergent behaviors.

Zombie Apocalypse

An agent-based model simulating the spread of a zombie outbreak through a population, exploring epidemic dynamics and containment strategies.

Ising Model

An model simulating the behavior of a magnetic system, exploring phase transitions and critical phenomena.

Langevin Dynamics

A simulation of Langevin dynamics, modeling the motion of particles in a fluid under the influence of friction and random forces.

Ideal Gas

A simulation of Ideal Gas behavior, modeling the motion of gas particles in a container.

Diffusion-Limited Aggregation

A simulation of Diffusion-Limited Aggregation, modeling the growth of fractal structures through the random movement and sticking of particles.

Bernoulli Percolation

A simulation of percolation theory, modeling the spread of a fluid through a porous medium.

Paper Recreations

Attempts at independent implementations of published research in complex systems and active matter. Each card links directly to the public repository containing the numerical methods and source code.

Physics Informed Neural Networks

Original: M. Dagrada, "Introduction to Physics-informed Neural Networks," Towards Data Science, 2022



Demonstrates how a neural network can solve a differential equation with very little training data by incorporating the equation directly into the loss function.

View Repository

Bacterial Growth and Dormancy

Original: M. S. Svenningsen, N. Mitarai, "Simple bacterial growth model for the formation of spontaneous and triggered dormant subpopulations," Physical Review Research 6, 033072, 2024

Develops a simple bacterial population model showing how growth dynamics and environmental fluctuations can spontaneously produce dormant subpopulations, while external perturbations can also trigger dormancy.

View Repository

Semi-classical Game of Life

Original: D. A. Faux, P. Bassom, "The game of life as a species model," Am. J. Phys., 2023



Extends Conway’s Game of Life into a generalized cellular-automaton model where species are characterized by preferred neighborhood liveness and environmental resilience, allowing qualitative modeling of extinction, recovery, and predator–prey dynamics.

View Repository

Non-Reciprocal Dense Particles

Original: J. U. Klamser, L. Berthier, "Directed percolation transition to active turbulence driven by non-reciprocal forces," arXiv preprint, 2025



Dense particle systems with non-reciprocal interactions undergo a directed-percolation transition from an absorbing state to chaotic, turbulence-like dynamics as non-reciprocity increases

View Repository

Self Propelled Particles

Original: N. van den Berg, K. Thijssen, T. T. Nguyen et al., "Emergent collective alignment gives competitive advantage to longer cells during range expansion," Nature Communications 17, 1039, 2026

Agent-based models to show that longer bacteria outcompete shorter ones during colony expansion through emergent collective alignment and mechanical exclusion at the expanding front
