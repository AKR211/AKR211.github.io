1st Summer Project: Computational Studies of the Ising Model

  • Date: 2/6/2023 - 31/7/2023
  • Location: NISER, Bhubaneswar
  • Guide: Dr. A. V. Anil Kumar

We conducted a study of the two-dimensional Ising model implemented using the Metropolis Algorithm to observe parameters such as Energy, Net Magnetization, Specific Heat Capacity, Magnetic Susceptibility etc. and determined the existence of a phase transition in the model. Using Finite size scaling and the Binder Cumulant, we determined the Critical Temperature of the phase transition which was in agreement with the analytical value. We further briefly analyzed the 2D model under an external magnetic field and the three-dimensional Ising Model to find an increase in the Critical Temperature. Semester Project: Predicting Protein Oligomericity from Amino Acid Sequence

  • Date: 2/1/2024 - 7/9/2024
  • Location: NISER, Bhubaneswar
  • Guide: Dr. Subhankar Mishra
  • Collaborators: Joel Joseph K. B.

The oligomerization state of protein sequences is crucial for understanding their function and stability, impacting proteomics research and drug development. This study proposes a machine learning approach to predict the oligomerization states of protein sequences from their amino acid sequences. A large dataset from the RCSB Protein Data Bank was cleaned and balanced for training. We implemented and fine-tuned various machine learning models, such as k-Nearest Neighbors (kNN), Multilayer Perceptron (MLP), Recurrent Neural Network (RNN), Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM), Transformers (Tfmr), and Mamba, to predict eight oligomerization states.The kNN model consistently outperformed others, reaching an accuracy of up to 68.80% on the original dataset. Among deep learning models, LSTM performed best. Although promising, deep learning models need improvement in generalization. Future work will focus on hyperparameter tuning, alternative feature spaces, and evaluating additional metrics to enhance model performance and generalization. This study is the first to apply deep learning methods to this specific problem, paving the way for future research in predicting protein oligomerization states.

2nd Summer Project: Predicting the Stress Tensor for a simulated molecular system

  • Date: 14/5/2024 - 14/7/2024
  • Location: IISER, Kolkata
  • Guide: Dr. Neelanjana Sengupta
  • Collaborators: Pallab Dutta

This study describes the development and analysis of models for computing the stress tensor in a water box simulation. The stress tensor computation is inherently difficult, with a time complexity of O(n4), where n is the number of atoms. Our dataset includes 5001 instances, with each frame of simulation comprising of 4275 atoms and each having 8 input and 6 output features. We use MultiLayer Perceptrons (MLP) and Graph Neural Networks (GNN) to model the stress tensor based on atomic positions, velocities, and one-hot atom type encodings. Despite its ability to recognise non-linear patterns, the MLP overfits and does not generalise well to the test set. In contrast, GNN models, which are intended to capture local atomic interactions via message passing, perform better with more balanced training and test results. Our findings show that GNNs are an interesting approach to stress tensor computing in molecular dynamics simulations, although more enhancements are required to attain optimal performance metrics.
